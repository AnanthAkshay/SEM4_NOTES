Complete solutions with definitions, formulas, and step‑by‑step calculations.

📖 Definitions (Q1 – Q13)

1. Define sampling distribution of means.
The sampling distribution of the mean is the probability distribution of all possible sample means of a given sample size \(n\) drawn from a population. Its mean equals the population mean \(\mu\), and its standard deviation (standard error) is \(\sigma/\sqrt{n}\).
2. Define Standard error.
Standard error (SE) is the standard deviation of a sampling distribution. For the sample mean, \(SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\) (or \(s/\sqrt{n}\) when \(\sigma\) is unknown).
3. Define confidence interval.
A confidence interval is an interval estimate for a population parameter, computed from sample data, with a specified probability (confidence level) that the interval contains the true parameter. For mean: \(\bar{x} \pm z_{\alpha/2} \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\) or \(\bar{x} \pm t_{\alpha/2} \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\).
4. Define null hypothesis and alternate hypothesis.
  • Null hypothesis (\(H_0\)): A statement of no effect or no difference, assumed true until evidence suggests otherwise.
  • Alternate hypothesis (\(H_1\) or \(H_a\)): The statement that contradicts \(H_0\), indicating an effect or difference.
5. (i) Type I and Type II errors   (ii) Testing of hypothesis.
  • Type I error: Rejecting a true null hypothesis. Probability \(\alpha\) (level of significance).
  • Type II error: Failing to reject a false null hypothesis. Probability \(\beta\).
  • Testing of hypothesis: A statistical procedure to decide whether to accept or reject a null hypothesis based on sample evidence.
6. (i) Level of significance   (ii) One‑tailed and two‑tailed tests.
  • Level of significance (\(\alpha\)): Probability of making a Type I error, typically 0.05 or 0.01.
  • One‑tailed test: Rejection region is in one tail of the distribution (directional hypothesis).
  • Two‑tailed test: Rejection regions are in both tails (non‑directional).
7. Define student’s t‑distribution.
The t‑distribution is a continuous probability distribution used when the population standard deviation is unknown and the sample size is small. It is symmetric about zero, bell‑shaped, but with heavier tails than the normal. Its shape depends on degrees of freedom \((n-1)\).
8. Define F‑distribution.
The F‑distribution is the distribution of the ratio of two independent chi‑square variables divided by their degrees of freedom. Used to compare variances and in ANOVA. It is skewed to the right and depends on two parameters: numerator df and denominator df.
9. State the condition when F‑test is used.
The F‑test is used to test whether two population variances are equal (\(H_0: \sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2\)). It requires independent random samples from normal populations.

11. Define Chi‑square distribution.
The chi‑square (\(\chi^2\)) distribution is the sum of squares of independent standard normal variables. It is used for goodness‑of‑fit tests, tests of independence, and variance estimation. Its mean = df, variance = 2df.
12. Write formula for χ²‑test and explain the terms involved.
\[ \chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i} \] where \(O_i\) = observed frequency, \(E_i\) = expected frequency under \(H_0\). The test statistic follows \(\chi^2\) with \((r-1)(c-1)\) degrees of freedom for contingency tables, or \(k-1\) for goodness‑of‑fit.
13. Population: 3,7,11,15. Samples of size 2 with and without replacement. Find (a) population mean, (b) population SD, (c) mean of sampling distribution of means, (d) standard error of means.
Population: \(N=4\). Values: 3,7,11,15.
(a) \(\mu = \frac{3+7+11+15}{4} = \frac{36}{4}=9\).
(b) \(\sigma^2 = \frac{(3-9)^2+(7-9)^2+(11-9)^2+(15-9)^2}{4} = \frac{36+4+4+36}{4}= \frac{80}{4}=20\), so \(\sigma = \sqrt{20}=4.4721\).
(c) Mean of sampling distribution of means = \(\mu = 9\) for both cases.
(d) With replacement: \(SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{4.4721}{\sqrt{2}}=3.1623\).
Without replacement: \(SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\frac{N-n}{N-1}} = 3.1623 \times \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}} = 3.1623 \times 0.8165 = 2.582\).
μ=9, σ=4.472, mean of sample means=9, SE (with)=3.162, SE (without)=2.582

1. A sample of 900 members is found to have a mean of 3.4 cm. Can it be reasonably regarded as a truly random sample from a large population with mean 3.25 cm and SD 1.61 cm?
\(H_0: \mu = 3.25\), \(H_1: \mu \neq 3.25\). Large sample, use z-test. \[ z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} = \frac{3.4 - 3.25}{1.61 / \sqrt{900}} = \frac{0.15}{1.61/30} = \frac{0.15}{0.05367} = 2.795 \] Critical value \(z_{0.025}=1.96\). Since \(|z| = 2.795 > 1.96\), reject \(H_0\). The sample cannot be regarded as a random sample from the given population. z=2.795, reject H₀ → not a random sample.
2. A population consists of the four numbers 2, 3, 6, 8, 11. Consider all possible samples of size 2 drawn (i) with replacement and (ii) without replacement. Find (a) population mean, (b) population standard deviation, (c) mean of sampling distribution of means, (d) standard error of means.
Population: 2,3,6,8,11. \(N=5\).
(a) \(\mu = \frac{2+3+6+8+11}{5} = \frac{30}{5}=6\).
(b) \(\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x_i^2}{N} - \mu^2 = \frac{4+9+36+64+121}{5} - 36 = \frac{234}{5} - 36 = 46.8 - 36 = 10.8\). So \(\sigma = \sqrt{10.8} = 3.2863\).
(c) Mean of sampling distribution of means = \(\mu = 6\) for both cases.
(d) With replacement: \(SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{3.2863}{\sqrt{2}} = 2.324\).
Without replacement: \(SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{N-n}{N-1}} = 2.324 \times \sqrt{\frac{5-2}{5-1}} = 2.324 \times \sqrt{\frac{3}{4}} = 2.324 \times 0.8660 = 2.013\).
μ=6, σ=3.286, mean of sample means=6, SE(with)=2.324, SE(without)=2.013.
3. A sample of 100 days is taken from meteorological records of a certain district and 10 of them are found to be foggy. What are the probable limits of the percentage of foggy days in the district?
Sample proportion \(p = 10/100 = 0.10\). Standard error \(SE = \sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.10 \times 0.90}{100}} = \sqrt{0.0009} = 0.03\).
For 95% confidence limits: \(p \pm 1.96 SE = 0.10 \pm 0.0588 = (0.0412, 0.1588)\) → \(4.12\%\) to \(15.88\%\).
For 99% confidence limits: \(p \pm 2.576 SE = 0.10 \pm 0.0773 = (0.0227, 0.1773)\) → \(2.27\%\) to \(17.73\%\).
95%: (4.1%, 15.9%); 99%: (2.3%, 17.7%).
4. The following data represent biological values of protein from cow’s milk and buffalo’s milk at a certain level. Cow’s milk: 19, 17, 15, 21, 16, 18, 16, 14; Buffalo’s milk: 15, 14, 15, 19, 15, 18, 16. Examine if the average values significantly differ.
Let sample 1 (cow): \(n_1=8\), sample 2 (buffalo): \(n_2=7\).
\(\bar{x}_1 = \frac{19+17+15+21+16+18+16+14}{8} = \frac{136}{8}=17\).
\(\bar{x}_2 = \frac{15+14+15+19+15+18+16}{7} = \frac{112}{7}=16\).
\(s_1^2 = \frac{\sum(x_1-17)^2}{7} = \frac{(2)^2+(0)^2+(-2)^2+(4)^2+(-1)^2+(1)^2+(-1)^2+(-3)^2}{7} = \frac{4+0+4+16+1+1+1+9}{7}= \frac{36}{7}=5.1429\).
\(s_2^2 = \frac{\sum(x_2-16)^2}{6} = \frac{(-1)^2+(-2)^2+(-1)^2+(3)^2+(-1)^2+(2)^2+(0)^2}{6} = \frac{1+4+1+9+1+4+0}{6}= \frac{20}{6}=3.3333\).
Pooled variance: \(s_p^2 = \frac{(n_1-1)s_1^2 + (n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1+n_2-2} = \frac{7×5.1429 + 6×3.3333}{13} = \frac{36+20}{13}= \frac{56}{13}=4.3077\). \(s_p = 2.0755\).
\(t = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{s_p \sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1}+\frac{1}{n_2}}} = \frac{17-16}{2.0755 \sqrt{\frac18+\frac17}} = \frac{1}{2.0755 \times 0.5175} = \frac{1}{1.074} = 0.931\).
df = 13, critical \(t_{0.025,13}=2.160\). Since \(|t| = 0.931 < 2.160\), accept \(H_0\). No significant difference.
t=0.931, accept H₀ → means are not significantly different.
5. Show that 95% confidence limits for the mean μ of the population are \(\bar{x} \pm \frac{t_{0.05} s}{\sqrt{n}}\). Deduce that for a random sample of 16 values with mean 41.5 inches and sum of squares of deviation from mean = 135 inches², the 95% confidence limits are 39.9 and 43.1 inches.
For small samples, when population σ is unknown, the confidence interval is \(\bar{x} \pm t_{\alpha/2, n-1} \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\). With 95% confidence, \(\alpha=0.05\), so \(t_{0.025, n-1}\). Usually denoted \(t_{0.05}\) in some texts for two‑tailed? The given formula uses \(t_{0.05}\) meaning \(t_{0.025}\)? We'll verify with numbers.
Given: \(n=16\), \(\bar{x}=41.5\), \(\sum (x-\bar{x})^2 = 135\). Then \(s^2 = \frac{135}{15}=9\), so \(s=3\).
\(t_{0.025,15}=2.131\). Margin = \(2.131 \times \frac{3}{\sqrt{16}} = 2.131 \times 0.75 = 1.59825\).
Confidence limits: \(41.5 \pm 1.598 = (39.902, 43.098) \approx (39.9, 43.1)\). Hence proved.
CI = (39.9, 43.1).
